I've been drawing one new 16x16 sprite every morning and studying old NES
sprite sheets to get better at it. Ten sheets from Super Mario Bros. 1 & 3,
Mega Man 2, Kirby's Adventure, Bubble Bobble, and Zelda. Here is what keeps
showing up.

**Blocking beats outlining.** Small Mario is 16x16 with zero outline: red
cap and shirt, skin face, brown sideburns. Color segments alone define every
part. Wheelie, the tire enemy from Kirby, reads as a circle with two shades
and no outline on the curve. At this size, an outline costs pixels you need
for the actual shape.

**Every detail earns its place.** Mario's eye is two stacked dark pixels.
The mustache exists to separate the nose from the face — a pragmatic fix,
not decoration. Mega Man's helmet fin is two or three pixels and reads
instantly. Bubble Bobble enemies are single-color bodies with a face made of
four or five pixels.

**Variants change one thing.** Raccoon, Tanooki, and Frog Mario keep the
whole body and add a single feature: a tail, ears, a suit color. Sir Kibble
is a Waddle Doo blob plus a helmet and a blade. When drawing variants,
resist redesigning everything.

**3-4 colors, flat, one shade for volume.** The NES palette budget was real.
Mega Man's cyan armor shades to darker blue on one side only. Zelda's
overworld enemies run on two or three colors: Octorok's snout is a 2-pixel
protrusion, Tektite's legs are 1-pixel lines.

**Pick one outline convention per piece.** Super Mario Bros. 1 uses heavy
dark outlines; Zelda items use none. Mixing the two in one drawing looks
indecisive. Decide before you start.

**Silhouette first.** Zelda's sword is a 2-pixel-wide vertical blade, the
key is a shaft with teeth, the raft is a slatted rectangle. If the
silhouette needs explaining, the design is wrong. Kirby's Waddle Dee is an
orange oval, two 1-pixel eyes, feet — nothing else.

**Water and texture are controlled speckle.** Zelda's water is darker blue
pixels scattered on mid blue: dense enough to read as texture, sparse enough
not to become noise. That is what restrained dithering looks like.

That is the whole list. Blocking, a 3-4 color budget, one convention, a
silhouette that needs no explanation, and every pixel earning its place.
